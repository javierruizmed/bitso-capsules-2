This capsule focuses on a specific question the Investments Fleet asked: how did the Kobra In-App Flow release on May 7 change the contact rate for Stocks Account Limits? Before this release, users who wanted to increase their Stocks trading limits had to coordinate a physical visit through an agent — a manual, friction-heavy process where most users simply stopped responding. This capsule measures what changed, and what friction remains.
01Context — The Process Before and After

By regulation, Stocks limit increases in Mexico require identity verification through a physical visit — a representative visits the user's home to complete the required paperwork. Before May 7, this entire process was coordinated manually by the CS through CS tickets.

03Stocks Form — The "Not Scheduled" Story

The Stocks operations form provides the clearest before/after signal. Before May 7, the dominant status was "Not Scheduled" — users who had been contacted to coordinate the Kobra visit but never responded. After the release, users schedule directly in the app, so this status category collapsed almost entirely.

🔴 "Not Scheduled" dropped 88% — from 199 in Wk19 to 23 in Wk20. Confirmed by the CS: this is entirely attributable to the In-App Flow release. 05What Users Are Telling Us — Residual Friction Post-Release

The In-App Flow eliminated the coordination burden. But CS still receives contacts — and they now represent the cases where the new process breaks down. The three tickets selected for the Live Ticket Review illustrate the three residual friction patterns:

🔴 The visit was scheduled — but no one showed up

Ticket #3341090 — User successfully scheduled the Kobra visit through the app, but the representative never arrived. The user followed the new process correctly and was left waiting with no resolution and no follow-up communication.

This is execution failure on the provider side — the In-App Flow worked, but Kobra didn't deliver on its commitment. From the user's perspective, the outcome is the same as before: no limit increase, no explanation.

🟠 No coverage in the user's area

Ticket #3339062 — User attempted to schedule through the app but Kobra has no coverage in their geographic area. The case is now handled manually by the CS via DHL — the same labor-intensive process as before, just for a smaller subset of users.

This is a geographic gap in the Kobra network. The DHL rise in Wk20–21 (0 → 7 cases) confirms this pattern is emerging as the In-App Flow scales. Without coverage expansion, users outside Kobra's network remain on the old process indefinitely.

🟠 The visit can't be at the user's workplace

Ticket #3343313 — User wants the visit at their office, but the process requires a home address. The user was not informed of this restriction before scheduling — they went through the full scheduling flow only to find out at the end that their preferred location is not valid.

This is a UX gap: the restriction on visit location is not communicated clearly during the scheduling process. Users discover it after investing time in the flow, generating a CS contact that could be prevented with a single line of copy in the right place.

🏠 The physical visit itself — a process users don't want

Beyond the specific failure cases above, the CS notes a consistent pattern in user feedback: many users express frustration with the requirement to receive a physical visit at all. Users question why a digital financial platform requires someone to come to their home — especially when the process can take days to coordinate, requires being available at a specific time, and may not be compatible with their lifestyle or living situation.

This is a structural friction that the Kobra In-App Flow addresses partially — by making scheduling easier — but does not eliminate. The underlying requirement of a physical visit remains. As the platform scales and user expectations around fully digital experiences increase, the physical verification requirement is worth a proportionality review: is it the minimum necessary to meet regulatory requirements, or can any part of the process be digitized without compliance risk?

💡 What could help: Four targeted improvements to close the residual friction: (1) a Kobra SLA and reliability review to address execution failures; (2) a coverage map or upfront check before the user starts the scheduling flow, to redirect DHL-zone users immediately; (3) clear communication of visit location requirements (home address only) at the start of the scheduling flow, not at the end; (4) a proportionality review of the physical visit requirement — is it the minimum necessary for compliance, or can any part of the process be digitized?
