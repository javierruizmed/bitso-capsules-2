03Kobra In-App Flow — Update

We shared a dedicated Kobra capsule with the squad earlier this month. The In-App Flow launched May 7 and drove an 88% drop in "Not Scheduled" tickets that same week — a significant improvement that held through May. June shows volume returning as new users enter the process, and DHL cases are rising in areas where Kobra has no coverage.

One additional signal worth sharing: on June 8, a member of the CS team went through the Kobra visit process to increase their own limit. The experience raised some concerns we'd like to flag.

🟠 The visit experience — firsthand account

The scheduling window was 3 hours, and the representative arrived late. He came in a personal vehicle with no uniform or visible identification, and began photographing the house and electricity meter on arrival. Signing was done on the user's own device. During the visit, the representative was on his personal phone asking colleagues what documentation was still needed. The scheduling flow did not communicate upfront that the visit had to take place at a personal residence.

We raise this not to escalate, but because it helps explain the drop-off rate the data shows. For users who complete the In-App scheduling and then experience this, the friction hasn't gone away — it's moved to a different part of the process.

🟠 Service quality — DHL agent behavior

Two users — one Premium Bit and one Has Been Premium Bit — escalated complaints in May about the behavior of DHL representatives during document collection visits.

🔴 Proof of Address — the validation gap

Proof of Address has been the largest single friction point in Stocks account opening across all five months. The pattern observed in May tickets points to a specific recurring scenario: users upload a valid proof of address document, but the address on it doesn't match the address registered in their Bitso profile. The document is rejected, and the user receives an automatic email from Bitso — triggered by internal Zendesk rules — letting them know the verification couldn't be completed and inviting them to contact chat.

This is by design: when the KYC API creates a ticket and auto-resolves it, Bitso sends that automatic message to provide a faster path to resolution. The intent is good — chat can solve the issue in the first contact. The gap is in what the email communicates: it tells users to reach out, but not why the document was rejected or what to fix before resubmitting. The agent in chat identifies the mismatch and guides the user to update their address — the interaction resolves, but it shouldn't require that step.

The scale of this is larger than CS incoming alone reflects. The table below shows auto-solved API validator tickets by rejection reason. Proof of Address accounts for the vast majority — 2,418 of 2,791 auto-solved tickets in May (87%).

An open question for the squad: is the address mismatch requirement communicated clearly enough during the upload flow itself — or does the user only find out after the rejection email?

🟠 PEP declaration — growing and structural

PEP-related contacts have grown steadily from 73 in January to 126 in May. Unlike Proof of Address, which can be resolved once the user understands the mismatch, PEP errors often create a hard block — an incorrect declaration prevents account activation entirely. Three confirmed cases from the previous period showed users completely blocked with no self-correction path available. The previous capsule flagged a confirmation modal as a potential intervention. We'd like to understand whether anything has changed in this flow since then.

🟠 Account limits — volume down significantly

Account limits tickets dropped from 286 in January to 72 in May. The reduction reflects process improvements in how limit increases are coordinated. Residual volume represents cases that still require manual handling.

05Voice of the Customer

Survey feedback for April and May shows a high proportion of promoters — the 67.7% CSI in May reflects genuine satisfaction with agent resolution. What the detractors surface is worth attention: the frustration isn't usually with the agent, it's with a process that requires too many steps, too many document resubmissions, and too little feedback when something fails.

👎 Detractors — documentation loop

"En mucho falta de mejorar, tengo un mes tratando de activar mi cuenta y cada día meter otra vez mis documentos. A la hora que termino todo el proceso se brinca y me pide otra vez mis documentos y aparte piden mi comprobante de domicilio que está en mi INE y otra vez empezar de nuevo. Nunca hay ningún asesor de servicio al cliente, puro bot." #3316619

👍 Promoters — agent resolution works, product gaps remain visible

Most promoters in May credited the support team for resolving their issue. A recurring suggestion across both months points to a product gap rather than a support issue: users want clearer portfolio performance data and more transparency in how investment results are displayed. These aren't CS friction points, but they reflect product expectations that aren't being met.

"Poner el gráfico exacto de lo que se está ganando en una inversión en las acciones, no el acumulado — eso dificulta la inversión."

07What Users Are Telling Us — And What Could Help

The account-opening flow is generating contacts that agents resolve well — the CSI reflects that. What the data points to is friction that sits upstream of CS: users who don't understand why their document was rejected, or who restart a multi-step process without knowing what went wrong the first time.

✅ Agent macro for Kobra escalation — addressed

In May, it was identified that agents were not consistently directing users to the In-App Kobra flow when they reached their limit. A new macro was implemented to ensure agents guide users to schedule through the app first — before escalating to the manual process. This should reduce unnecessary tickets.

📋 Clearer rejection feedback on Proof of Address

The current rejection email tells users their verification failed and to contact CS. What it doesn't tell them is why. In the cases reviewed, the reason was an address mismatch — something the user could fix themselves if they knew what to look for. A rejection message that identifies the specific issue (and ideally links to the profile section where it can be corrected) would reduce a significant share of the contacts this category generates.
