Account Limits
KYC Verification Friction · Dec 2025 – Apr 2026 · Retail Segment
Prepared by Javier Ruiz · Experience Senior Specialist · Bitso Customer Service  ·  #exp-retail-user-voice-capsule
This capsule covers Account Limits — a category where the user's ability to operate on Bitso is directly tied to completing identity verification successfully. When the verification journey breaks, users don't just experience friction: they hit a hard stop that prevents them from accessing higher limits and transacting freely. The data points to a persistent and significant volume of users stuck in this flow, with IDWall/Jumio — Bitso's biometric and document verification provider — appearing as a recurring point of failure for both legitimate users and, as documented in Operation Restituição, as an exploited gap for organized fraud.

CSAT004 — Document Clarity: an active initiative addressing this

The Registration squad has an active initiative — CSAT004: Document Clarity Upgrade — that directly targets the high rejection rate at the document submission step. The problem definition aligns precisely with what CS sees in ticket volume:

📋 CSAT004 — What it addresses

Problem: High rejection rate in Account Limit Increase requests due to users uploading invalid or incomplete documentation. Users rely on vague instructions ("upload a document like a payslip or bonus receipt") with no visual examples or clear validation rules — leading to incorrect uploads, multiple rejections, and back-and-forth tickets.

CS impact: Every rejected document creates at least one follow-up ticket. The current absence of clear requirements is a direct driver of the Proof of Income and document-related tickets visible in the incoming data.

Proposed improvements: Clear bullet-point documentation requirements per market (MX, AR, BR), visual examples of valid vs. invalid documents, multi-document upload support, and removal of "account statement" as a valid option across all markets.

Expected KPI impact: Reduction in first-time rejection rate, lower ticket volume for documentation issues, faster approval cycle, CSAT improvement.

03Root Cause — The Jumio Problem

Jumio powers the ID / Liveness Test step — the single largest document failure in Account Limits. When Jumio underperforms, users hitting the verification wall have no alternative path. CS cannot override Jumio results or manually verify identity — they can only ask users to retry. The event calendar shows two incidents in 60 days.

04Pain Points
🔴 Jumio failures block the highest-intent users with no alternative path

The ID / Liveness Test step generates 386–619 tickets per month. When Jumio experiences delays or errors, CS has no resolution tool — they can only ask the user to retry. There is no degraded-mode experience, no estimated wait time, and no alternative verification path for users whose liveness check fails due to provider issues rather than genuine identity concerns.

These are users who have actively chosen to upgrade — they're at the highest-intent moment in the Bitso journey. The drop-off at this stage represents lost activation, not just a support ticket volume problem.

🔴 Upgrade 1→2 is the most common breaking point

Level 1→2 consistently generates the most tickets of any upgrade tier — peaking at 856 in March. This is the first significant limit increase after basic account setup, and it sets the tone for the entire relationship. Users who fail here are unlikely to attempt further upgrades. It's worth reviewing whether the document requirements at this tier are proportional to the limit being unlocked.

🟠 Unique Identifier failures — a separate, persistent structural issue

The Unique Identifier sub-category (CURP in Mexico, CPF in Brazil) generates ~350 tickets/month regardless of Jumio status — a separate problem with identifier validation. At this volume, it's the second-largest document failure after ID/Liveness and deserves its own investigation. Users who submit identifiers that don't match expected formats or names get rejected with no clear guidance on what's wrong or how to fix it.

🟠 Authentication failures bleeding into the verification journey

Based on cross-referencing with Guardians capsule data, there is a compounding pattern affecting some users: the same SMS and email delivery failures documented in the Guardians capsule also impact the Account Limits flow. Users who need to verify their identity to upgrade limits may simultaneously be unable to authenticate — creating a double barrier that CS cannot resolve end-to-end. This is inferred from the Guardians data and the timing of both contact type spikes in March–April, not from direct Account Limits verbatims.

05Operation Restituição — The Other Side of the Jumio Gap

The IDWall verification gaps that block legitimate users from upgrading their limits were simultaneously exploited by an organized fraud group. In March–April 2026, a coordinated IRS refund scam attack used biometric verification weaknesses to open 500+ fraudulent accounts and drain real users' tax refunds. This is documented in an internal cross-department investigation — Operation Restituição — produced by KYC, Fraud, Product, Engineering, Compliance, and CS teams.

Three attack vectors — all exploiting IDWall gaps

🔴 M.O. 01 — Face swapping / Deepfake liveness (from March 20)

Attackers overlaid a real victim's face onto their own biometric video using real-time face-swap tools. IDWall's liveness detection failed to identify the manipulation and auto-approved the check. One extreme case: the exact same liveness was approved across 52 different accounts — no deduplication existed in the pipeline.

🔴 M.O. 02 — CPF/biometric identity mismatch (from March 27)

Scammers registered any CPF in Hubble and validated a completely different person's biometrics in IDWall. No crossmatch existed between the two systems and no API ticket was generated — so the account passed as fully verified with a mismatched identity. 143 confirmed repeated-face static photo upload cases were documented.

🟠 M.O. 03 — Delete-on-rejection + IRS timing (from April 7)

When IDWall rejected an attempt, attackers immediately deleted the account — erasing their trail and preventing auditing. The operation was deliberately timed around the Receita Federal IRS refund disbursement window to maximize financial impact per account.

The KYC blind spot — a systems gap, not a human one

The KYC team was operating correctly within the information they received. The problem was structural: accounts auto-approved by IDWall via face swap or liveness bypass never generated a KYC review ticket — meaning they passed as fully verified with zero human review at any point.
