Local Bank Transfer Issues · Dec 2025 – Apr 2026 · Retail Segment
Prepared by Javier Ruiz · Experience Senior Specialist · Bitso Customer Service  ·  #exp-money-movement-capsule
This capsule covers Withdrawals — consistently one of the top contact types in CS — with a focus on Local Bank Transfer (MXN/Local Currency) issues. The data reveals a category with persistently low satisfaction, driven primarily by fraud-related account blocks that leave users unable to access their funds with little to no communication. A March spike in both volume and fraud blocks, combined with a growing SMS authentication failure pattern, makes this one of the most urgent experience gaps in the platform.
01Incoming Overview

Withdrawals represents between 12% and 18% of total CS incoming — the third largest contact type after Access to my account and Update my account data. The category spiked notably in March 2026, reaching its six-month peak of 4,434 tickets (18% of total CS), before partially recovering in April.

This is a data quality gap with real operational implications.

What's inside "Other"? — A closer look

The "Other" root cause bucket deserves its own analysis. Historically stable at 68–160 tickets/month, it spiked to 368 in April — a 425% increase from March. Cross-referencing with the event calendar, the most likely explanation is the SPEI liquidity shortfall on April 26–27, which fully disabled MXN withdrawals for users without triggering a standard Fraud, KYC, or Compliance block. A liquidity-driven outage doesn't fit any of the existing categorization buckets, so these tickets fall into "Other" by default.

This points to two gaps: a product gap (no user communication when SPEI liquidity is insufficient) and a data gap (no specific categorization for system-level blocks vs. user-level blocks). Until "Other" is broken down further, CS cannot accurately report on what's actually blocking users in April — and Product cannot prioritize correctly.

⚠️ Recommendation: Add at least two new Level 4 sub-categories: "System/Infrastructure block" (for liquidity outages, SPEI downtime, processor failures) and "Undetermined block" (genuinely unknown). This split would allow CS to distinguish operationally-caused blocks from unclassified ones, and would have immediately flagged the April liquidity event in the data.
02Root Cause — What Drove the March Spike

The March 2026 spike in withdrawal tickets was not a single event — it was the result of at least 7 distinct incidents plus 4 maintenance windows converging within the same month. The combination of SPEI infrastructure issues, Banxico maintenance, a new security requirement, and SMS delivery failures created a perfect storm for the withdrawal flow.

2 Mar
SPEI problem Incident
Opening incident of the month — SPEI transactions affected from day two.
5 Mar
Step Up in Withdrawals Release
New security requirement added to the withdrawal flow. Users now need to complete an extra verification step — adding friction to an already strained process, contributing to "I can't withdraw" and "My withdrawal was rejected" tickets.
17 Mar

Scheduled downtime on the primary SPEI connectivity layer.
22 Mar
Banxico infrastructure issues affecting withdrawals Incident
Central bank-level disruption directly impacting MXN withdrawal processing.
24–27 Mar
SMS delivery failure — users can't complete withdrawal verification Incident
Four consecutive days of SMS delivery failure. Users who needed SMS to verify withdrawals were completely blocked. Directly explains the "I haven't received the SMS code" sub-issue spiking from 25 tickets in Feb to 106 in March.
28–29 Mar
Banxico weekend maintenance + Rejected MXN withdrawals Maintenance + Incident
Weekend maintenance followed immediately by a wave of rejected MXN withdrawals on the 29th.
ℹ️ April added a critical new event: On April 26–27, a liquidity shortfall in the SPEI account fully disabled MXN withdrawals. This is likely the primary explanation for the "Other" root cause bucket exploding to 368 tickets in April — a liquidity-driven block doesn't map cleanly to Fraud, KYC, or Compliance categories, so it falls into "Other." The April 8 Withdrawal Service outage and 408 SPEI withdrawals stuck in processing on April 15 also contributed to the continued elevated volume.

Survey that measures the experience of the user with Bitso on a scale from 1 to 10. Sent once the ticket is set to solved.

Survey that measures the experience of the user with the CS agent on a scale of 👍 / passive / 👎. Sent once the ticket is set to solved.

05Pain Points
🔴 Fraud blocks: effective security, zero user communication

The root cause analysis shows that Blocked by Fraud is responsible for the majority of "I can't withdraw" tickets — 781 in March alone (the peak). The problem is not the block itself — fraud prevention is necessary — but the complete absence of communication when it happens. Users discover they can't access their funds with no explanation, no estimated timeline, and no proactive outreach from Bitso.

The result is the most damaging user experience pattern in this data: a user with frozen funds, no resolution, and a CS team that can only say "it's under review." This drives both the lowest CSI scores and the most hostile verbatims in the dataset.

⚠️ The March fraud wave (781 blocks) appears to have been resolved by April (234 blocks) — but the experience cost was high. The CSI did not recover proportionally, suggesting lasting trust damage beyond the incident itself.
🔴 "Other" block category — 368 unclassified cases in April

While fraud blocks decreased sharply in April, the "Other" root cause category exploded from ~70 tickets to 368 — a 425% increase. This means a significant and growing number of users are being blocked for reasons that are either not yet categorized or don't fit existing definitions. Without knowing what's driving these blocks, both CS and Product are operating blind on one of the largest emerging issues in the category.

🟠 SMS authentication failures blocking withdrawals

The SMS delivery problem — documented in detail in the Guardians capsule — is now intersecting with the withdrawal flow. "I haven't received the SMS code" within Bank Transfer tickets grew from 9 in December to 137 in April (+1,422%). For users trying to make a financial transaction, an authentication failure that can't be bypassed is categorically worse than the same failure in a login context: the user's money is actively inaccessible until the issue is resolved.
